Transformation Is Not Always Beautiful

I went to Ecuador expecting transformation.

The journey was part of a six-month program focused on purpose, power, pleasure, service, and intentional living. We would travel deep into the Amazon, spend time with Indigenous communities, participate in ceremonies, and learn from women who had dedicated their lives to creating change.

It sounded meaningful, spiritual, and maybe even magical.

Some of it was.

There were pink river dolphins, brightly colored birds, sacred rituals, long conversations, and moments when the rainforest felt alive with God.

There were also bugs, mud, sleepless nights, hunger, fear, cold showers, outhouses, and long stretches without any connection to my family. I was hot, uncomfortable, exhausted, and completely out of control.

That was where the real learning began.

It is easy to talk about surrender when we are comfortable. It is easy to believe we are patient when no one is testing our patience. It is easy to imagine ourselves as generous, brave, and spiritually grounded when we have everything we need.

But discomfort has a way of telling the truth.

Ecuador exposed the part of me that wanted to be transformed, but not too disrupted. I wanted to serve, but I also wanted to feel safe. I wanted adventure, as long as someone could assure me that nothing would go wrong. I wanted to become a more evolved version of myself without having to meet the frightened, frustrated, controlling parts of me along the way.

But those parts were not interruptions to the journey.

They were the journey.

Real transformation is rarely as polished as it looks afterward. It can be confusing, embarrassing, exhausting, and deeply inconvenient. Sometimes we do not recognize growth while it is happening because it feels more like falling apart.

Before we can change, we have to be willing to see ourselves honestly.

Not only the generous parts.

Not only the brave parts.

All of it.

What discomfort in your life might be revealing something you are finally ready to see?
