What I Should Have Learned in Kindergarten but Learned at 47

Before entering the Amazon, I packed milk for my coffee.

I knew our supplies would be limited, and I was worried there would not be enough. Coffee is part of my morning ritual, and milk felt like one small comfort I could preserve in a place where nearly everything else would be unfamiliar.

So I kept it for myself.

I did not share it.

By the time we reached our next lodge, the heat and altitude had spoiled every bit of it.

My milk had gone sour.

The lesson was almost too obvious.

In trying to make sure I had enough, I had kept something from others and ended up with nothing. What could have been shared was wasted.

I remember thinking, Tasha, did you learn nothing in kindergarten?

Apparently, I had not.

Scarcity does strange things to us.

When we are afraid there will not be enough, we hold tighter. We protect our portion. We convince ourselves that sharing now might leave us without what we need later.

We do this with far more than food or money.

We withhold encouragement because someone else's success makes us question our own.

We hold back ideas because we want the credit.

We protect our time, attention, connections, and resources because we are afraid giving them away will somehow diminish us.

But so often, what we refuse to share does not become more valuable. It simply goes unused.

Generosity is not only about how much we have. It is about how we relate to what is already in our hands.

Sometimes we wait to give until we have more money, more time, more influence, more confidence, or more certainty. But generosity almost always begins before we feel completely ready.

A meal becomes nourishment.

An introduction becomes an opportunity.

An encouraging word becomes courage.

A small gift becomes evidence that someone has not been forgotten.

The sour milk taught me something I should have learned long before I was 47.

Protecting everything does not guarantee abundance.

Sometimes abundance begins the moment we open our hands.

What are you holding that was meant to be shared?
