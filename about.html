The origin

A fledgling idea: what if the softest flutter of a wing could set off a chain reaction of generosity?

Tasha wanted to start a micro-philanthropy movement that combined Mahatma Gandhi's popular concept, "Be the change you want to see in the world," with Edward Lorenz's Butterfly Effect theory: that even the softest flutter of a wing can affect the molecules around it, setting off a chain reaction that produces major change.

The result was The Butterfly Effect, founded in 2013 with a mission to promote contagious generosity through interactive art in communities all over the world.

The movement's first giving activity, Butterfly Drops, began inspiring people to give in ways that were both unexpected and fun. In 2016, Tasha innovated the idea into Social Butterfly art installations in public spaces, fanning waves of social-media giving for a wide variety of causes.

Today, through thousands of Butterfly Drops and hundreds of Social Butterflies around the world, the movement has raised nearly $320,000 for over 500 charitable organizations, empowering countless individuals to make a difference.
